Privacy Policy — Unwind

Effective date: September 6, 2026

Unwind is an Android app that asks you to trace a path with your finger before a distracting app opens. This policy explains, in plain words, what the app does and does not do with information. The short version: your data stays on your phone. The app has no internet permission at all, so it cannot send anything anywhere, even by mistake.

The accessibility service

The app lock works through an Android accessibility service. When you switch apps, Android tells the service the technical name of the app now on screen (for example, com.instagram.android) — nothing more.

  • The service compares that name, on your phone, against the list of apps you chose to lock. If it matches, the trace screen opens.
  • It reads no screen content, no text, no messages, no passwords and no screenshots — only the app's package name. The service is configured with canRetrieveWindowContent="false", which means Android does not hand it the contents of any window, and it listens for one kind of event only: a window coming to the front.
  • The name is never sent anywhere. There is no "anywhere": the app has no server and no permission to reach one.

So that one trace covers one visit, the service also holds two things in its memory while it is running: the name of the app you are in right now — a single name, replaced when you move to another app — and a note that you have already traced for an app, which expires on its own, so that opening a keyboard or a photo picker inside that app does not ask you to trace a second time.

To tell "you switched to another app" apart from "a keyboard opened on top of the app you were already in", the service asks Android which of your installed apps are launchers, which are keyboards, and which have a home-screen icon. Those lists describe the apps on your phone, not you, and they are worked out afresh each time the service starts.

None of that is written to storage and none of it leaves the phone. It lives in the running app's memory and is gone when the service stops.

You turn this service on yourself in Android's accessibility settings, after the app explains it, and you can turn it off there at any time.

What the app stores on your phone

Everything below lives only in the app's private storage on your device, in a database called unwind.db. We have no copy and no way to read it.

  • The apps you chose to lock — each one's package name, the label Android shows for it, the mode you picked, and when you added it.
  • Your settings — whether you have finished onboarding, the master blocking switch, how long an app stays open after a trace, the path width, and the wall's sound.
  • Your deck of cards — for each card you drew or received, its name, its colour, the stroke itself as a list of points, whether you drew it or were sent it, and when it arrived.
  • Your walked diary — one row for each path you traced: which drawing or card it was, when you traced it, and which locked app was waiting behind the gate. This is a history of when you opened the apps you chose to lock. It is kept so the drawings can be redrawn faintly on your home screen; it is stored on your phone and nowhere else.
  • Whether you bought the app or are still inside the free trial, and when that trial started and ends.

Deleting it: uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android settings, permanently deletes all of it. There is nothing to delete anywhere else.

Android's own backup

The app allows Android's standard backup, which means your settings, your deck and your walked diary may be copied into your own Google account's app backup, and restored when you set up a new phone or reinstall the app. That backup is a feature of Android between you and Google under Google's privacy policy — it does not go to us, and we cannot read it. You can turn app backup off for the whole phone, or for this app, in Android's system settings.

What uses the internet

Nothing in the app does. Unwind does not declare the INTERNET permission, so Android will not let it open a network connection at all. Not for analytics, not for crash reports, not for anything. Every path, card and drawing is generated or read on the phone itself.

That leaves one thing, and it happens outside the app:

  1. Google Play purchases. The one-time purchase and the trial are handled by Google Play, in Google's own app and servers. Payment details go to Google, never to us — Unwind only learns "purchased" or "not purchased" and stores that flag on the phone. Google's own privacy policy governs the transaction.

Sending a card

You can send a card you drew to someone else. When you do, the app writes that single card to a temporary file and hands it to Android's share sheet, and you choose where it goes. The file holds the drawing and its name — nothing about you, your phone or the apps you lock. The app never shares anything on its own, and never more than the one card you picked.

What the app does not do

  • No accounts and no sign-in.
  • No analytics, no trackers, no advertising and no ad SDKs.
  • No selling, sharing or transmitting of personal data — the app has no server to send it to and no permission to reach one.
  • No reading of notifications, messages or screen content.
  • No location, no contacts, no camera, no microphone.

Permissions, in full

The app declares three permissions and nothing else:

  • Accessibility service — described above; the app lock depends on it.
  • Vibrate — the trace answers your fingertip with small taps.
  • Billing — lets Google Play handle the one-time purchase.

Children

Unwind is made for adults and is not directed at children under 13. Regardless of age, the app collects nothing.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever starts doing something this policy does not cover, the policy will be updated first and the effective date above will change.
